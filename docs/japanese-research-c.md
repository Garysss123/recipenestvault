# Japanese batch C research and photo provenance

Research date: 2026-08-29. This record covers all seven batch-C recipes and their manually reviewed photographs: `miso-soup`, `onigiri`, `tamagoyaki`, `chawanmushi`, `zaru-soba`, `kitsune-udon`, and `shoyu-ramen`.

## Editorial and method standard

Each recipe below was cross-checked against independent HTTPS recipe sources and then rewritten into an original five-language method. Quantities are standardized for home cooking, and the method states the relevant stock, cooking, food-safety and doneness cues. The photo is treated as a finished-dish reference, not as evidence of a site test or one continuous cooking session.

## Miso soup

### Recipe sources cross-checked

- [Just One Cookbook — Homemade Miso Soup](https://www.justonecookbook.com/homemade-miso-soup/): dashi made with kombu and katsuobushi; miso is dissolved at the end and the soup is not boiled after miso is added; tofu and wakame are the visible core ingredients.
- [Japanese Cooking 101 — Miso Soup Recipe](https://www.japanesecooking101.com/miso-soup-recipe/): home-style dashi/miso sequence, tofu and wakame handling, and serving guidance.

The published method uses 700 ml water, 8 g kombu, 12 g katsuobushi, 250 g silken tofu cut into 3 cm cubes, 4 g dried wakame and 60 g awase miso. It explicitly accounts for the 30-minute kombu soak and five-minute wakame soak, strains the dashi, warms tofu/wakame below a boil, dissolves miso off heat, and serves at approximately 70–75°C. The recipe does not add scallion because the selected photograph has no scallion garnish.

## Onigiri

### Recipe sources cross-checked

- [Just One Cookbook — Onigiri Recipe (4 Easy Fillings)](https://www.justonecookbook.com/onigiri-rice-balls/): short-grain rice cooking, light salting, gentle triangle shaping and nori wrapping; the page notes that fillings are optional.
- [Japanese Cooking 101 — Onigiri (Japanese Rice Balls)](https://japanesecooking101.com/rice-ball-onigiri-recipe/): plain rice-ball preparation, shaping and nori use.
- [Kikkoman — Onigiri](https://www.kikkoman.com/en/cookbook/washoku/onigiri.html): an independent washoku reference for salted rice balls and nori wrapping.

The published method uses 300 g Japanese short-grain rice, 360 ml cooking water, 1.5 L rinsing water, 4 g fine salt and two sheets of plain roasted nori. Its 65-minute timing includes ten minutes of draining, twenty minutes of pre-soak, roughly seventeen minutes of covered cooking and ten minutes of covered rest before cooling and shaping. It makes six loose, unfilled triangles and wraps nori immediately before serving. For safety, rice held longer than two hours is refrigerated, then reheated until steaming hot and 74°C at the centre before cooling to a warm shaping temperature. No hidden filling, sesame garnish or side dish is claimed because none is visible in the selected photograph.

## Tamagoyaki

### Recipe sources cross-checked

- [Just One Cookbook — Classic Tamagoyaki](https://www.justonecookbook.com/tamagoyaki/): thin egg layers, low/medium heat, rolling technique and a 74°C safety check adapted to this home batch.
- [Japanese Cooking 101 — Tamagoyaki Recipe](https://www.japanesecooking101.com/tamagoyaki-recipe/): staged pouring, folding/rolling and pan control for a Japanese rolled omelette.

The published method uses 300 g egg, 30 ml unsalted dashi, 18 g sugar, 2 g salt, 10 ml light soy sauce and 15 ml neutral oil. Each thin layer is rolled while glossy, then the finished roll is cooked until the thickest centre reaches 74°C with no liquid egg. The recipe is intentionally plain because the selected photograph shows only yellow layered egg.

## Chawanmushi

### Recipe sources cross-checked

- [Just One Cookbook — Chawanmushi (Japanese Savory Egg Custard)](https://www.justonecookbook.com/chawanmushi-savory-steamed-egg-custard/): egg-to-dashi custard ratio, straining, covered steaming and gentle heat.
- [Japanese Cooking 101 — Chawanmushi (Savory Egg Custard)](https://www.japanesecooking101.com/chawanmushi/): traditional cup steaming sequence and mushroom/herb filling options.

The published method uses 150 g egg, 450 ml cooled unsalted dashi, 10 ml light soy sauce, 10 ml mirin, 2 g salt, 40 g shiitake, 40 g enoki and 8 g mitsuba. It steams covered at 85–90°C, verifies a 74°C centre with no liquid egg, and serves at 65–70°C. It deliberately excludes uncertain seafood, chicken, kamaboko and ginkgo rather than guessing from the photograph.

## Zaru soba

### Recipe sources cross-checked

- [Just One Cookbook — Zaru Soba (Cold Soba with Dipping Sauce)](https://www.justonecookbook.com/zaru-soba-cold-soba-noodles/): homemade mentsuyu, a large pot of unsalted water, boiling time, cold rinsing/rubbing, ice chilling and separate dipping presentation.
- [Japanese Cooking 101 — Zaru Soba Recipe](https://japanesecooking101.com/zaru-soba-recipe/): basic cold-soba preparation, sauce separation and serving on a bamboo zaru.

The published method uses 320 g dried soba, 4 L unsalted boiling water, separate cold rinse and ice water, kombu/katsuobushi mentsuyu with sake, mirin and soy sauce, plus shredded nori and sliced scallion. It calls for fully tender but still chewy noodles, cold rinsing until the starch is gone, thorough draining, and dipping the noodles in separate cooled sauce instead of pouring it over.

## Kitsune udon

### Recipe sources cross-checked

- [Just One Cookbook — Kitsune Udon](https://www.justonecookbook.com/kitsune-udon/): seasoning and simmering aburaage, clear dashi broth, frozen udon timing and the tofu-pouch origin of the dish name.
- [Japanese Cooking 101 — Kitsune Udon Recipe](https://www.japanesecooking101.com/kitsune-udon-recipe/): blanching the fried tofu, sweet-savoury pouch simmer, dashi seasoning and bowl assembly.

The published method uses 800 g frozen udon, 240 g aburaage, separately measured 1 L blanching water and 2 L noodle water, sweet soy-mirin tofu seasoning, 1 L unsalted dashi, a clear soup seasoning and the photographed narutomaki, scallions and tenkasu. It cooks the noodles separately until fully hot and springy, keeps the dashi below a hard boil, and serves at 70–75°C.

## Shoyu ramen

### Recipe sources cross-checked

- [Just One Cookbook — Shoyu Ramen (Recipe from Bizentei, Tokyo)](https://www.justonecookbook.com/shoyu-ramen/): soy-based ramen tare, broth/noodle/topping assembly and the distinction between tare and soup.
- [The Kitchn — Shoyu Ramen Recipe](https://www.thekitchn.com/how-to-make-the-best-ramen-at-home-236345): home ramen workflow, chicken stock option, chashu and topping preparation.
- [Japanese Cookbook — Weeknight Shoyu Ramen](https://japanesecookbook.com/recipes/shoyu-ramen/): practical weeknight proportions using explicitly unsalted chicken stock and a separate soy tare.

The published method deliberately uses a listed 1.2 L unsalted chicken stock rather than pretending to make a twelve-hour tonkotsu broth. It braises and temperature-checks pork chashu, makes 150 ml soy tare separately, boils 600 g fresh ramen in a separate 3 L pot until fully cooked and springy, and serves the photographed dark clear broth with two thick chashu slices per bowl, menma, narutomaki and sliced scallions. The selected photograph has no egg, nori, corn or sesame, so none is claimed in the visual brief.
