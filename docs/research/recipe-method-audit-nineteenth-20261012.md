## Findings and changes

| Existing recipe | Finding | Change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ethiopian Doro Wat | The 1.2 kg onion base and egg finish had no complete time sequence, the chicken had a single estimate without a timed recheck, and six servings did not allocate the six eggs and eight injera rounds. | Keep six servings. Set prep/cook/total to 50/120/170 minutes. Refrigerate the lemon-and-salt chicken for 30 minutes while preparing onions. Dry-cook onions for 25–35 minutes; simmer chicken about 45–60 minutes and start checking at 45 minutes at the thickest point, away from bone, for 74°C / 165°F, continuing in 5-minute intervals if needed. Hard-cook eggs for 10–12 minutes while the stew simmers, then finish them in the sauce for 10 minutes and rest the stew for 10 minutes. Divide the 1.5 kg raw bone-in chicken evenly (about 250 g per person before cooking), give one egg per diner, and share the eight injera rounds as one each plus two for the table. |
| Filipino Chicken Adobo | The chicken marinade was reserved but never explicitly returned to the pot; batch-browning lacked useful timing, and the 900 g chicken and 600 g cooked rice were not divided among four servings. | Keep four servings. Set prep/cook/total to 40/55/95 minutes. Keep the 30-minute marinade chilled, brown in two batches for 3–4 minutes per side, boil the vinegar and water for 3 minutes, then cook the reserved soy-garlic marinade with the chicken for 30–35 minutes. Check the thickest piece away from bone at 74°C / 165°F and continue in 5-minute intervals if needed. Prepare the 600 g cooked rice during the braise and divide it into four 150 g bowls; distribute the chicken and sauce evenly (about 225 g raw bone-in chicken per serving before cooking). |
| Uzbek Plov | The 100 ml oil and 12 g salt had no stage-by-stage allocation. The 700 ml water plus unspecified additions, lamb braise, rice steam, and total cook estimate did not provide enough sequencing or doneness cues; six servings were not portioned. | Keep six servings. Set prep/cook/total to 30/125/155 minutes. Soak and drain rice for 20–30 minutes while preparing the ingredients. Allocate the 100 ml oil as 60 ml for lamb, 20 ml for onion, and 20 ml for carrots. Use 8 g salt in the zirvak and the remaining 4 g in the separate rice water. Simmer the lamb gently for 45–60 minutes until nearly fork-tender; use all 700 ml listed water in the zirvak and add measured 50 ml splashes only if the liquid drops below halfway up the meat. Add separate hot water to reach about 1 cm above the rice, cook uncovered 8–12 minutes, then cover and steam 15–20 minutes until the grains are tender without a hard centre and the liquid is absorbed; rest covered for 10 minutes. Divide six ways (about 83 g raw lamb and 67 g dry rice per serving). No fixed internal temperature was added to the long braise of 3 cm lamb cubes. |
| Jamaican Jerk Chicken | The 25 ml oil was not allocated between paste and grill, grill-zone times were not stated, and the poultry endpoint lacked a timed recheck or a four-way cut allocation. | Keep four servings and set prep/cook/total to 260/45/305 minutes. Use 20 ml of the listed oil in the paste and reserve 5 ml for the grate. Refrigerate the chicken for at least 4 and up to 24 hours. Allow about 10 minutes to set up the two-zone grill, sear over direct heat for 10–15 minutes, then cover and finish over indirect heat for about 15–20 minutes. Check the thickest thigh away from bone at 74°C / 165°F; continue in 3-minute intervals if needed. Rest for 5 minutes and divide the eight pieces into four servings of two (about 300 g raw chicken per serving before cooking, bones included). |

## Recipe references checked

These references informed dish identity and method comparison. Their formulas and times differ from the site's amounts and editorial timings; the report does not claim that this site's recipes were prepared by those authors.

- Doro Wat: [Food Network — Doro Wat (Stewed Chicken with Berbere and Eggs)](https://www.foodnetwork.com/fnk/recipes/doro-wat-stewed-chicken-legs-with-berbere-and-eggs-10035303) and [Koki Afrique — Ethiopian Doro Wat](https://kokiafrique.com/en/dishes/doro-wat/). They support the berbere-onion base, chicken-and-egg stew, and injera service; their onion and braise times differ from this site's larger onion amount.
- Filipino Chicken Adobo: [Panlasang Pinoy — Filipino Chicken Adobo](https://panlasangpinoy.com/filipino-chicken-adobo-recipe/) and [Food Network — Filipino Chicken Adobo](https://www.foodnetwork.com/recipes/filipino-chicken-adobo-recipe-1955818). These support the soy, vinegar, garlic, pepper, bay, browning, braising, and rice context. The references describe different vinegar timing and formulas; the site keeps its own sequence and amounts.
- Uzbek Plov: [Food Perestroika — Traditional Uzbek Plov](https://foodperestroika.com/2013/10/18/traditional-uzbek-plov/) and [Milk Street — Plov with Chicken](https://www.177milkstreet.com/recipes/plov-chicken). Food Perestroika's recipe supports the meat, rice, carrot, onion, spice, liquid-absorption and covered-rest context. Milk Street's accessible page excerpt identifies plov as an Uzbek rice-and-meat dish and recommends soaking and rinsing rice; the full directions require membership and were not used for timing or ratios.
- Jamaican Jerk Chicken: [Food Network — Jerk Chicken](https://www.foodnetwork.com/recipes/ree-drummond/jerk-chicken-3329367) and [Bon Appétit — Jamaican Jerk Chicken](https://www.bonappetit.com/recipe/jamaican-jerk-chicken). These support jerk seasoning, chilled marination, direct/indirect grilling, and the 10–15 plus 15–20 minute grill stages. Their ingredients and cut sizes differ from this site's.

## Official food-safety reference

- [FoodSafety.gov — Cook to a Safe Minimum Internal Temperature](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures): poultry thighs and legs, 74°C / 165°F; untreated eggs, cook until yolk and white are firm. The Doro Wat, adobo, and jerk chicken edits apply the poultry endpoint to the thickest piece and do not treat the source recipe's time as a safety guarantee. The lamb entry in the chart is for steaks, roasts, and chops; this audit left the 3 cm lamb cubes on a long, gentle braise with a fork-tender endpoint instead of adding an unrelated fixed temperature.
