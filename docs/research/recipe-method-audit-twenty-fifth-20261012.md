## Findings and changes

| Existing recipe | Finding | Change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Katsudon (`katsudon`) | Bowl portions and the dry-rice timing assumption were implicit. The pork cutlets and egg topping did not state separate applicable safety endpoints. | Keep 2 servings and 25/30/55-minute prep/cook/total estimates. Start from 360 g cooked rice, 180 g per bowl, and add the separate rice-cooking time if starting dry. Season each 180 g cutlet with 1 g salt and 0.25 g white pepper. Fry at 175°C; check each 1.5 cm whole-cut pork cutlet from the side at 63°C / 145°F, then rest 3 minutes. Split the egg topping into two 75 g shares, cook in two additions, and set to 71°C / 160°F with no liquid egg. Serve each bowl with one cutlet, half the onion broth, one egg share, and 5 g mitsuba. |
| Tom yum goong (`tom-yum-goong`) | The recipe lacked clear per-serving shrimp and stock amounts. Shrimp doneness needed a species-appropriate visual check rather than an unrelated fixed temperature. | Keep 4 servings and set prep/cook/total to 25/32/57 minutes. Divide the 500 g shell-on raw shrimp into four 125 g portions and chill below 4°C with separate utensils for cooked soup. Use 900 ml strained stock, or 225 ml starting stock per serving before other ingredients. Poach about 2–3 minutes until the thickest shrimp is firm, pearly, and opaque throughout with no grey translucent centre; stop promptly. Allocate the finished 200 g mushrooms as about 50 g per serving. |
| Avgolemono chicken soup (`avgolemono`) | The sequence left the chicken cooling and broth/rice preparation as separate waits. Starting broth and per-bowl chicken, rice, egg, and lemon shares were unclear; egg tempering needed a safe endpoint without boiling. | Keep 6 servings and set prep/cook/total to 30/75/105 minutes. Check the thickest thigh near the bone at 74°C / 165°F, extending in 5-minute increments if needed. Cool the chicken for 10 minutes while straining and measuring 1.8 L broth. Cook 150 g rice in that broth for 15–17 minutes: 25 g dry rice and about 300 ml starting broth per serving. Shred chicken while the rice simmers. Temper the egg-lemon mixture, then stir over very low heat to at least 71°C / 160°F while keeping it below 80°C. Serve six bowls with about 83 g chicken, 25 g dry-rice equivalent, half a large egg, and 15 ml lemon juice per bowl. |
| Apple pie (`apple-pie`) | Filling amounts and overlapping waits were not fully sequenced; the bake finish depended too much on time alone, and the long cooling period before clean slices needed to be accounted for. | Keep 8 servings and set prep/cook/total to 320/70/390 minutes, with the 4-hour minimum set time included in the displayed total. Chill pastry for 60 minutes while preparing the apples; macerate the seasoned filling for 20 minutes during that chill. State the full apple seasoning amounts. Bake at 220°C for 20 minutes, then at 190°C for about 40–50 minutes as a guide, checking for a deep-golden crust and vigorous bubbles through the centre vents. Shield a darkening rim with foil. Cool for at least 4 hours before cutting eight equal slices, about 175 g raw prepared apples represented per slice. No internal safety temperature was added to this pie. |

## Official food-safety guidance

These official sources are separate from the dish references:

- [FoodSafety.gov — Safe Minimum Internal Temperatures](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures): whole-cut pork at 63°C / 145°F followed by a 3-minute rest; poultry at 74°C / 165°F; egg dishes at 71°C / 160°F.
- [FDA — What You Need to Know About Egg Safety](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/what-you-need-know-about-egg-safety): egg dishes at 160°F / 71°C.
- [USDA FSIS — Correct Food Thermometer Placement](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/news-events/news-press-releases/do-you-know-correct-place-insert-your-food-thermometer): thermometer placement guidance for thin foods; the pork method specifies side insertion.
- [FDA — Selecting and Serving Fresh and Frozen Seafood Safely](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/selecting-and-serving-fresh-and-frozen-seafood-safely): shrimp is done when firm, pearly, and opaque.
