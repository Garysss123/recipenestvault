## Findings and changes

| Existing recipe | Finding | Change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Japanese chicken curry rice (japanese-curry) | The method listed 400 g cooked rice but did not divide it among four servings. Rice and curry shared a generic two-hour cooling window even though cooked rice has its own handling guidance. | Divide rice into four 100 g portions; allocate each plate 100 g cooked rice, about 75 g raw-chicken equivalent in curry, and 20 g fukujinzuke. The chicken endpoint remains the poultry-specific 74°C / 165°F. The card now cools rice quickly in shallow portions, ideally refrigerates within one hour, uses it within 24 hours, and reheats it only once until steaming throughout. Cool curry separately within two hours; the three-day curry limit is explicitly this recipe's conservative choice. |
| Spaghetti alle vongole (spaghetti-alle-vongole) | Live-clam handling and the distinction between shellfish doneness and leftover reheating needed to be explicit. The method also did not allocate the listed ingredients to four bowls. | Buy tagged, already-purged live clams from a reputable supplier; keep at or below 4°C / 40°F, discard cracked and tap-tested non-closing shells, and scrub under cold running water. Treat 3–5 minutes as a guide, remove clams as shells open, and discard those still closed; shell opening is the relevant cooking cue, so no unrelated thermometer target is imposed. Divide into four warm bowls with 90 g dry spaghetti and about 250 g shell-on clams per serving before discards. Leftovers go into a shallow container within two hours and are reheated to at least 74°C / 165°F; two days is a conservative recipe limit. |
| Arroz con leche (arroz-con-leche) | The original method left cooked rice pudding at room temperature for 45 minutes before chilling, and the displayed prep estimate did not distinguish cooling and refrigerated setting from active preparation. | Divide into six shallow 180 ml dishes, use an ice-water bath, and refrigerate when heavy steam subsides, ideally within one hour. About 15 minutes for the ice bath is a planning estimate, not an official threshold; the card follows the FSA rule to cool cooked rice quickly and ideally refrigerate within one hour. Chill at least one further hour before serving. Cold service is the default; if warmed, reheat once until steaming throughout. Set prep/cook/total estimates to 90/115/205 minutes to include estimated rapid cooling and the one-hour minimum chill. The 24-hour use window and one-reheat rule follow the FSA's cooked-rice guidance. |
| Full English breakfast (full-english-breakfast) | Multiple pans were described as sequential steps, obscuring how the side dishes finish while the sausages roast. Soft-yolk eggs needed a clear pasteurization caveat, and the four-plate allocation was unspecified. | Coordinate beans with the oven, tomatoes and mushrooms in two skillets, then black pudding and bacon in separate skillets, followed by bread and eggs. Check the largest sausage at the centre for the official 71°C / 160°F ground-meat endpoint; hold early-finished components at or above 60°C / 140°F. Soft yolks use refrigerated pasteurized shell eggs; ordinary eggs are cooked until whites and yolks are firm. Allocate two sausages (about 120 g), two bacon rashers (about 60 g), two black-pudding slices (about 60 g), one egg, 75 g mushrooms, 100 g tomato, 100 g beans, and one thick 40 g bread slice per plate. The coordinated 20/45/65-minute display is an estimate; leftover storage uses a separately labelled conservative three-day limit. |

Portion equivalents, the 15-minute ice-bath planning estimate, the full-English coordinated timing, and the two-day and three-day leftover limits are recipe estimates or editorial limits, not official values. The 90 g dry pasta portion is 360 g ÷ 4; the 250 g clam portion is 1 kg shell-on ÷ 4 before discards. The chicken equivalent is 300 g ÷ 4. Official temperatures and cooling advice are attributed separately.

## Official food-safety guidance

Official food-safety entries are separately labelled in each recipe's source data.

- [FDA — Selecting and Serving Fresh and Frozen Seafood Safely](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/selecting-and-serving-fresh-and-frozen-seafood-safely): shellfish tags and cold storage; clams are cooked when their shells open, and shells that remain closed are discarded.
- [FoodSafety.gov — Safe Minimum Internal Temperatures](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures): ground meat/sausage 71°C / 160°F and leftovers 74°C / 165°F. The live-clam cooking cue is shell opening rather than a forced thermometer reading.
- [FoodSafety.gov — 4 Steps to Food Safety](https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/4-steps-to-food-safety): refrigerate perishable food within two hours, keep the refrigerator at or below 4°C / 40°F, use shallow containers to cool food, and hold hot food at or above 60°C / 140°F.
- [UK Food Standards Agency — Home food fact checker](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/home-food-fact-checker/home-food-fact-checker): cool cooked rice quickly, ideally within one hour; refrigerate and use within 24 hours; reheat once until steaming hot throughout.
- [FDA — What You Need to Know About Egg Safety](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/what-you-need-know-about-egg-safety): refrigerate eggs, cook ordinary eggs until whites and yolks are firm, and use pasteurized eggs for recipes served undercooked.
- [FoodSafety.gov — Cold Food Storage Charts](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/cold-food-storage-charts): general refrigerator-storage reference. The shorter three-day English-breakfast limit is marked as a conservative recipe choice.
