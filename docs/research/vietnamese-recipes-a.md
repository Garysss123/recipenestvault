## Method reconciliation and visual acceptance criteria

### Beef pho

Sophie describes a long, gentle bone simmer with meat removed as it becomes tender and roasted aromatics and whole spices added toward the end. Vicky also separates tender meat from the continuing broth and strains before final seasoning. This edition chooses six hours of bone cooking, three litres of finished stock for six bowls, brisket plus thin sirloin, and modest southern-style herbs. Thin beef is cooked in the pot instead of relying on a bowl pour-over to make raw meat safe.

Photo: clear amber broth and flat white noodles with sliced beef, onion and green herbs. Sprouts and lime are included. Reject photos dominated by meatballs, tripe, unrelated noodles or red chile broth. Step scenes distinguish raw sirloin from cooked brisket and keep final bowls only in the serving step.

### Chicken pho

Both authors roast aromatics, briefly toast spices, poach chicken and cook noodles separately. Sophie returns the stripped bones to the broth; that gives this edition a richer broth without leaving breast meat in the pot for the entire stock period. This edition removes the spice pouch after the chicken is cooked and reheats the sliced meat just before serving. Four bowls use 2.4 litres of final broth.

Photo: light golden clear broth, flat noodles and poached chicken slices or shreds. Onion, scallion, cilantro and lime are disclosed. Optional hoisin and Sriracha side dips were added to match the photo review feedback; neither is a required ingredient in the clear broth.

### Bun bo Hue

Huy confirms separate beef and pork doneness times, annatto colour and round noodles. Colin Tu's method reinforces the long bone simmer, lemongrass, aromatic chile oil and shrimp-paste depth. This edition uses beef shank, pork hock and beef bones, removes tender pork first, and makes a manageable pot for six rather than scaling a restaurant pot by eye. The finished red aromatics stay in the broth while bones and long fibrous stems are removed.

Photo: red-orange broth, thick round noodles, beef shank and pork hock. The stated version has no blood cake, sausage or meatballs. Purple cabbage is the documented home substitute for banana blossom. A photo containing additional substantial meat toppings requires a replacement or a researched recipe correction.

### Bun rieu

Huy uses spooned crab-pork-shrimp mixtures; Vicky demonstrates crab and egg setting in the broth, tomato wedges and fried tofu. The standardized version combines a pork-rib broth with soft spooned crab, shrimp, pork and egg dumplings. Sixteen roughly four-centimetre dumplings are allowed to set undisturbed, then checked to 74°C. Tomatoes retain some visible wedges and tofu is added only in the final simmer.

Photo: tomato-red broth, thin round noodles, rustic crab dumplings or curds, golden fried tofu and tomato. Pork ribs are explicitly included. Snails, blood cake and sausage are not implicit in the dish and cannot be silently accepted in a photograph.

### Bun cha

Sophie gives caramel seasoning, fatty pork patties, a light dipping sauce and separate noodles. Vicky corroborates green-papaya pickle and patties served in their dipping bowl. Household choices of lemongrass vary; both consulted versions support it. This edition uses a one-hour refrigerated marinade, sixteen even patties and a quantified carrot-papaya quick pickle. All ground pork reaches 71°C.

Photo: grilled small flattened pork patties, clear amber dipping liquid, thin papaya-carrot pickle, separate noodles and leafy greens. Per photo-agent feedback, four fully cooked chả giò rolls are optional for four servings, with a separate 190°C reheating step to 74°C. Raw frozen rolls are explicitly outside that reheating instruction.

### Bun thit nuong

Huy specifies thin shoulder slices, a minimum four-hour marinade, batch grilling, herbs, peanuts and scallion oil. Jeannette supports the lemongrass, quick-pickle, noodle-salad and dressing workflow, while using a richer marinade. This edition keeps a simpler fish-sauce-based marinade, equal-thickness shoulder slices and separate dressing. The time budget includes four hours of refrigeration; pickle work happens in the last hour.

Photo: a dry noodle-salad bowl, sliced caramelized pork, cucumber, lettuce, carrot-daikon pickles, peanuts, scallion oil and herbs. Optional fully cooked chả giò rolls are included and reheated separately per photo-agent feedback. This is not a soup and not a plate of plain skewers without the noodle accompaniments.

### Goi cuon

Both authors support separate poaching, cooked shrimp halved lengthwise, thin pork, rice vermicelli, briefly moistened rice paper and restrained filling. Huy's page includes the hoisin-peanut sauce method and points out that wrappers soften after leaving water. This edition makes twelve rolls from eighteen shrimp (thirty-six halves), twelve noodle portions and twelve wrappers. Shrimp are cooked opaque; the numerical target is an additional conservative check. Optional cucumber and chives are fully disclosed.

Photo: translucent, soft, unfried rice-paper cylinders, visible pink shrimp and green herbs, with brown hoisin-peanut sauce. Crispy fried rolls are a different dish. Step scenes show the empty softened sheet, the partially folded filling and the final roll separately, without hands.

## Safety and localization checks

The [FoodSafety.gov temperature chart](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures), read on 2026-09-12, supports 74°C for poultry and leftovers and 71°C for ground pork. Long-simmered meat also needs the stated tenderness endpoint. This edition uses fully cooked beef and conservative whole-pork endpoints; the higher written targets are not represented as universal legal minimums. For shrimp, opaque flesh is retained as the principal visible endpoint, with 63°C as an additional numerical cooking check.

The [FoodSafety.gov storage chart](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/cold-food-storage-charts), read on 2026-09-12, supports chilled storage at 4°C or below and three-to-four-day cooked meat/soup limits. This edition uses three days for separated soup and meat, two days for assembled-meal components, and one day for fresh rolls because wrapper and herb quality decline rapidly. Shallow containers and prompt refrigeration are explicitly required.
