# Vietnamese recipe research — group C

Reviewed 2026-09-12. This record covers `ca-kho-to`, `thit-kho-trung`, `canh-chua-ca`, `che-ba-mau` and `banh-flan` in C1–C3. The recipes are original standardized home versions, not copies of one author's method and not a claim of Recipe Nest Vault kitchen testing.

## Standardization and appearance decisions

### Cá kho tộ — 7 steps

The site version uses four portions of 3 cm skin-on catfish steaks, 300 ml unsweetened coconut water and freshly made caramel rather than relying on dark soy sauce for colour. Initial simmering and later reduction follow the authors' low-agitation approach. Fish reaches 63°C, and the final sauce coats a spoon without burning. All fish sauce is in the marinade, all 300 ml coconut water is accounted for as 100 ml plus 200 ml, and reserved water is explicitly optional for loosening. Pepper is divided 1 g plus 1 g. Rice is a declared cooked accompaniment.

Finished photograph should show intact glazed fish steaks, brown sauce, scallion and pepper, with declared chile permitted. It must not show a tomato-based sauce or a materially different fish preparation.

### Thịt kho trứng — 8 steps

The chosen edition braises 1 kg pork belly with six eggs in a loose amber coconut-water sauce. Its 400 ml measured water is used as 30 ml plus 70 ml for caramel and 300 ml for the main braise. Fish sauce is divided 35 ml plus a final, taste-adjusted allowance of up to 15 ml. Eggs are boiled separately and added after the initial long pork simmer to avoid a rubbery white. The 70-minute and 20-minute stages have a clear tenderness endpoint and further ten-minute intervals if required; estimated timing may extend for a tougher cut.

Photo should show large pork-belly cubes and whole or halved eggs in a sauce that remains fluid, not an almost dry dark soy glaze. Rice is a declared cooked accompaniment.

### Canh chua cá — 6 steps

The pot uses 1.5 L soup water, split into 200 ml tamarind extraction and 1.3 L broth. An additional 1 L is explicitly for blanching the peeled edible stems and is discarded. Pineapple supplies sweetness, while tamarind gives the sour foundation. Fish and later vegetables have separate timing, and sprouts receive a final two-minute cook. Fish doneness is checked at 63°C.

Only culinary bạc hà sold as edible produce is intended; decorative or gathered elephant-ear plants are not a substitute. Wok and Kin explicitly sources these vegetables from the Asian-supermarket produce section and cooks the stems for ten minutes. The site method uses a five-minute blanch followed by five minutes in the soup. The Thai ingredient translation was corrected from “restaurant only” to the food-produce department, and Japanese rice-paddy herb now uses its identifiable loan name instead of an ambiguous water-parsley label.

Photo may show the pale porous stems along with tomato, pineapple, okra and sprouts in clear broth. Coconut milk and curry-like thickening are absent. The culinary note now explains how the stems absorb broth without referring to the implementation's photo-matching process.

### Chè ba màu — 9 steps

Both named authors present the familiar yellow mung-bean layer. The published site version clearly identifies itself as a whole-bean variation using cooked pale black-eyed peas; it does not claim that these sources used that exact bean choice. The traditional yellow-layer alternative remains quantified in substitutions. Cooked beans are required, including when red kidney beans are substituted; no raw-bean short cook is implied.

The 120 g sugar is fully accounted for as 40 g jelly, 25 g in each bean component and 30 g coconut sauce. The 200 ml syrup water is divided equally. Pure agar and green-coloured food-grade pandan extract are now specified so the stated set and colour are reproducible; sweetened jelly premix and colourless flavouring are not equivalent. Chinese step 8 formerly said “frozen”; it now correctly says refrigerated, set jelly, matching all other languages. Four glasses of at least 650 ml are specified to accommodate the generous bean, jelly, coconut and ice portions.

Photo shows red whole beans, a pale whole-bean layer, green pandan strands and white coconut sauce/ice. No yellow purée should be assumed in a whole-bean photo. Step counts and `imageScene` strings were preserved during this QA pass.

### Bánh flan — 8 steps

The custard uses 400 ml milk, 150 g condensed milk, three whole eggs and two yolks, divided into six 150 ml ramekins. It is strained and gently baked at 160°C to the written 80–82°C centre endpoint, then chilled at least four hours. The accompanying 90 ml coffee and 300 g ice equal the explicit 15 ml and 50 g per serving. These are optional additions to the finished custard, not undisclosed ingredients in the vanilla mixture.

The two source recipes use different dairy ratios, oven temperatures and coffee placement. The site version standardizes its own ramekin size, egg ratio, temperature and chilling period rather than implying it is an exact reproduction. The separate coffee-and-ice presentation has the additional primary support recorded above. Three-day chilled storage and no freezing are explicit. Product description wording was cleaned up to describe the optional serving style directly.

## Cross-collection QA results

All 21 current Vietnamese records were imported and reviewed for required five-language fields, numeric cooking values, ingredient allocation and major method transitions. Actual numeric time, quantity and temperature sets match across locales; remaining lexical differences are equivalent written-out counts versus Arabic digits, such as English “four” and Japanese `4`. Prep plus cook equals total for every recipe. The current collection has 172 steps, with counts from 6 through 11, and every step retains an English scene specification.

No step count or imageScene was changed by this independent QA pass. Only C2/C3 ingredient and localized copy corrections described above were made. B and C4 remained untouched because their owners were active.

Owner follow-up suggestions from the review: B's `banh-cuon` and `banh-mi-thit` call chả lụa “ham” in several localized labels; the ingredient is Vietnamese steamed pork sausage, and explicitly retaining `chả lụa` or a pork-sausage name would distinguish it from the separate pink ham in bánh mì. B's bánh xèo pork stage and C4's beef noodle-salad stir-fry give time and fully-cooked cues but no numeric fresh-meat endpoint; their owners can add a matching thermometer cue in all five languages without changing illustration scenes. These were not treated as proven unsafe methods, since the written methods already require cooked-through meat and the bánh xèo filling receives further cooking.
